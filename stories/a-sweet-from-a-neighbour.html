A Small Gift

Someone gave me some local sweets.

While staying in Kolkata, a local neighbour gave Tuzi some sweets to taste.

It was not a big event. There was no famous restaurant, no tourist attraction and no special ceremony. Just a local person sharing something from home.

But these are often the moments that survive longest in a traveller's memory.

I may forget the name. I won't forget the gesture.

I may not remember every name of the sweets I tasted. But I remember that someone decided to give them to me.

That is another side of travelling: sometimes you do not collect a souvenir. Someone gives you a memory instead.

Then I Walked Outside

This was the Kolkata I actually saw.

Kolkata was not only the places listed in a guidebook. It was also the streets between them.

Houses stood close to the road. Iron grilles, balconies, painted walls, tangled wires, narrow spaces, bicycles, motorcycles and Tuk-Tuks all became part of the ordinary scenery.

Nothing here was presented for a tourist. It was simply everyday Kolkata continuing around me.

A city is also made of ordinary moments.

A sweet from a neighbour. A house beside the road. A balcony, a wire, a Tuk-Tuk passing by.

None of these things needed to become an attraction. They were already part of the city.

And because I was there, walking through it, they became part of my 306-day memory too.
