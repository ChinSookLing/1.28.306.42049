After the Border, the Names

过了边境以后，看见那些名字

从 Sumilkino 边境出来之后，我第一次在 Iran 以后重新感到汽油便宜。可是那一天留下来的，不只是便宜汽油和免费停车，还有一路上那些战争纪念碑上的名字。

After leaving the Sumilkino border, it was the first time since Iran that petrol felt affordable again. But what stayed from that day was not only cheap petrol and free parking. It was also the names on the war memorials along the road.

2024 年 6 月 14 日早上，从 Russian APP Sumilkino border 出来之后，我终于真正进入 Russia 的路。

On the morning of 14 June 2024, after coming out from the Russian APP Sumilkino border, I finally entered the road inside Russia.

第一件让我很有感觉的事，是汽油价格。那是 Iran 以后第一次，我可以用大约 RM2.72 一 litre 的价格加 petrol。经历过欧洲一路的油价之后，这张小小的 receipt 变成了一种松一口气。

The first thing I really felt was the petrol price. It was the first time after Iran that I could fill petrol at about RM2.72 per litre. After the fuel prices through Europe, this small receipt became a kind of relief.

进入 Russia 后，我也很快注意到另一种感觉：这里的油站、大型 shopping mall、连锁超市，很多都可以 24/7 开着。那不是奢华，而是一种“任何时间都有物资”的安全感。

After entering Russia, I also noticed another feeling very quickly: petrol stations, large shopping malls, and chain supermarkets could stay open 24/7. It did not feel like luxury; it felt like the security of supplies being available at any hour.

然后是一餐很普通的 Russia 食物和汤，270 rub，大约 RM14。长途路上，这种普通的一餐很重要：热汤、马铃薯、茶，让身体慢慢知道自己已经过了边境。

Then there was a typical Russian meal with soup, 270 rubles, about RM14. On a long road, an ordinary meal like this matters: hot soup, potatoes, tea, and the body slowly understanding that the border was behind me.

那天晚上，我停在 Pskov Kremlin 附近的免费停车位。Toyotaki 停在路边，后面是城市、树和教堂的圆顶。刚刚经历过严格边境检查之后，可以停下来、睡一晚、不需要马上解释自己，是很珍贵的。

That night, I stayed in a free parking space near Pskov Kremlin. Toyotaki was parked by the road, with the city, trees, and church domes behind it. After the strict border inspection, being able to stop, sleep, and not immediately explain myself again felt precious.

6 月 15 日，我在 Velikiye Luki Park Parking 过夜。Toyotaki 停在树荫和阳光之间。刚从边境、汽油、超市和第一餐慢慢回到路上，停车位本身也变成那天的安定点。

On 15 June, I stayed overnight at Velikiye Luki Park Parking. Toyotaki was parked between shade and sunlight. After the border, petrol, supermarkets, and the first meal, the parking place itself became the stable point of that day.

后来我看见一座巨大的第二次世界大战纪念碑。最先震住我的，是它的钢铁感。那不是普通雕像的漂亮，而是一种从痛苦里锻出来的力量。

Later, I saw a huge Second World War memorial. The first thing that stunned me was the feeling of steel. It was not the beauty of an ordinary statue, but a strength forged out of pain.

我来自一个把和平当成最重要资产的国家，所以当我第一次站在那座巨大的 steel statue 前面时，我被它里面的 aura 震住了。过了这么多年，那种重量仍然还在。

I come from a country where peace is one of the most important assets we have. So when I first stood before that giant steel statue, I was stunned by the aura within it. Even after all these years, the weight was still there.

6 月 16 日，我在 Russia 的 Camping Parus, Staro 过夜。天色开始暗下来，Toyotaki 停在草地和树林旁边。那一夜比较安静，像是 Russia 第一段路在慢慢收尾。

On 16 June, I stayed overnight at Camping Parus, Staro, Russia. As the sky turned dark, Toyotaki rested beside the grass and trees. That night was quieter, as if the first Russia section of the road was slowly closing for the day.

可是 Russia 给我的第一天，不只是便宜 petrol 和一个可以过夜的位置。沿路我开始看到很多战争纪念碑。

But my first day in Russia was not only about affordable petrol and a place to sleep. Along the way, I began to see many war memorials.

有些纪念碑旁边是一排一排的名字。看久了，那些名字不再像一块碑上的字。那是别人家的儿子，别人家的丈夫，别人家的父亲。

Some memorials carried rows and rows of names. After looking at them for a while, the names no longer felt like words on a monument. They were someone’s son, someone’s husband, someone’s father.

我没有能力评判一个国家的全部历史，也不想把这段路写成政治判断。可是我站在那里时，最直接的感觉就是：战争留下来的不是数字，是家庭里少掉的人。

I could not judge the whole history of a country, and I did not want to turn this road into a political statement. But standing there, the feeling was simple: what war leaves behind is not only numbers. It is the missing people inside families.

那一天很奇怪。一边是刚过边境的轻松，一边是看到那些名字之后的沉重。Toyotaki 继续往前走，可是路边那些名字，让 Russia 的第一天不只是“我终于进来了”，也变成“我看见这里怎样记得失去的人”。

That day felt strange. On one side, there was the relief of getting through the border. On the other side, there was the heaviness of seeing those names. Toyotaki continued forward, but the names beside the road made my first day in Russia more than “I finally entered.” It also became “I saw how this place remembers the people it lost.”
